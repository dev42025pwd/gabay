## 2. Definitions

- **Metre cost:** the routing weight.
  - A walkway edge costs its length.
  - A walk-through-store edge costs its length × the published store weight (1.25 by default, a setting applied at publish, Blueprint §4.1; P0-03).
  - A floor change costs its connector's published metre cost, `CostBaseM + CostPerRiseM × rise` (L86, invariant 15).
  - Every cost is computed at publish (P0-03), and the engine reads it from the package.
- **Real metres:** the walked length, without weights. Legs show real metres (L92: "the steps show the real metres").
- **Route figure:** the metres shown on a way are its metre cost, as in the reference (`demo_app` `sheet.dart`, `routes[i].total`). Its minutes are metre cost ÷ 1.2 m/s (PRD §6.6, L86).
- **Leg:** a contiguous part of a route of one kind:
  - walk;
  - walk through a store (with the store);
  - ride (with the connector, up or down, the from level and the to level).
- **Shortcut edge:** a store-crossing edge of a store the mall marks `Unit.PassThrough` (L29, L92).
- **Passage:** a walk-through store that publish marks as the only way to one or more places (L92, Blueprint §4.1). If a place can be reached only through either of two stores, both are passages (P0-03 ruling D10). The package lists the places each passage serves.
- **Behind a passage:** a place is behind a passage when every way to it uses one of its passages.
- **No-stairs search:** the same search with every connector whose `CostModel` is `STAIRS` excluded.
- **Way:** one of the routes offered, either the lead way or, when one qualifies, the other way.
- **Venue frame:** the one planar coordinate frame, in metres, shared by every building and level of a venue (ruling E10).
- **Planar run** of an edge: the straight-line distance in the venue frame between its two end nodes.
- **Level span** of an edge: the absolute difference between the ordinals of its end nodes' levels. An edge with a level span above 0 is *level-changing*, whatever its kind: a connector, or a walkway between two buildings' levels with different ordinals (D6).
- **Estimate (RG2):** A*'s lower bound on the remaining metre cost (AC-36).

## 3. What the engine reads from the package (requirements on P0-03)

P0-06 does not fix the package's binary layout, because Spike 2 may change P0-03's format choices (intent P0-03, notes; ruling D1). The engine reads, through one package-reader interface:

1. **The graph:** a directed graph in CSR form. Each edge carries its metre cost, its real length and its kind:
   - walkway;
   - store crossing, with the store's unit;
   - connector, with the connector, its `CostModel`, its from level and its to level.
   A one-way connector has an edge only From → To (L86). A lift has an edge between every pair of stops it serves (Blueprint §3.4, `NavConnectorStop`).
2. **Per node:** its level and its planar position in metres, in the venue's one frame (ruling E10; P0-01 and P0-03 hold to it).
3. **Per level:** ordinal, building, `IsOutdoor`, name and short name.
4. **Per place:** its door nodes. **Per store:** `PassThrough`, its status's `IsRoutable` (a snapshot at publish; live status is P1-01), its name and its category icon.
5. **The passages:** each passage store and the places it serves.
6. **Connector names** (and `SpokenName`, read later by P0-14). Ruling A9 adds them to the package (P0-03 r2).
7. **The R\*Trees for RG3** (Blueprint §4.2): one over walkway edges and one over wall segments, per level. Ruling A9 adds the wall-segment R\*Tree to the package (P0-03 r2).
8. **The routing settings:** the venue tenant's values of the allow-listed `routing.*` keys (now only `routing.stairsSaveM`). Publish resolves them (the tenant's override, else the platform default) and they are fixed with the version (ruling A3; P0-03). `routing.*` never travels in the mall list. Everything else the phone needs (now `package.refreshCheckS`) travels only in the list (A3; P0-04).
9. **`PackageFormatVersion`.**

## 4. Acceptance criteria

### 4.1 The engine

AC-1: Given a package, an origin and a destination place, the engine returns the way with the least metre cost (L86, invariant 15). Its cost equals a plain Dijkstra's on the same graph within 0.001 m. Verify: unit (shared vectors, each also solved by a reference Dijkstra in the test).

AC-2: The search runs in a worker isolate that loads a package's graph once and serves every later query on that package. The UI isolate never runs a search, and reaches the engine only through asynchronous messages. Verify: unit (the service test counts graph loads across several queries and asserts the search code is called only in the worker).

AC-3: A destination with several doors is reached at whichever door gives the least metre cost (Blueprint §4.7, "a place's distance is its nearest door's"). Verify: unit (vector: two doors, the far door cheaper by graph).

AC-4: A one-way escalator is never travelled To → From (L86). Where the only short way runs against it, the engine returns the longer legal way. Where there is no legal way, it returns "no route". Verify: unit (two vectors).

AC-5: A lift is ridden between any two stops it serves, at the published cost of that pair (Blueprint §3.4). Verify: unit (vector: a lift with four stops; also V-RG2-2).

AC-6: A route can cross buildings and the venue's outdoor ground (whole site, L30). Every leg on an outdoor level is marked outdoor. Verify: unit (vector: two buildings joined by the outdoor ground).

AC-7: A* returns the same cost as the reference Dijkstra on every shared vector, including the RG2 vectors V-RG2-1 to V-RG2-6 (§4.6; L79 RG2 as amended by ruling A7; `<known_traps>`). These cover:
- an escalator with a horizontal run;
- a lift that spans several levels;
- a connector cost tuned below the defaults;
- a connector cost of 0;
- a store crossing, at weight 1.25 and below 1;
- a walkway between two buildings' levels with different ordinals.

On V-RG2-1, the test also runs A* with the Blueprint's literal estimate (floor-plan distance + the cheapest connector metre cost × levels apart) and expects its wrong answer, 31.66 m against the true 24 m. This proves the vector catches the trap. Verify: unit (vectors).

AC-8: The origin joins the nearest walkway edge that can be reached without crossing a wall, using the package's R\*Trees (L79 RG3; A9), not the straight-line nearest node. Verify: unit (vector: a corridor 1 m away through a wall and another 4 m away in the open; the route starts on the second).

AC-9: The route starts at the origin's projection on the snapped edge, not at either end node, and a one-way edge is joined only in its direction. Verify: unit.

AC-10: Every way carries its metre cost and its minutes (cost ÷ 1.2 m/s, PRD §6.6). Formats follow the reference (`demo_app` `app_state.dart` `fmtM` and the minutes formatter):
- Metres are rounded to whole metres and never shown below 1 m.
- Simple wording: "< 1 min" under 60 s, otherwise whole minutes rounded up.
- Technical wording: minutes with one decimal.

Verify: unit.

AC-11: The same package and query always give the same way, node for node. Equal-cost ties are broken by a fixed rule (DESIGN CHOICE, §7 DC-2). Verify: unit (100 repeats; tie vectors).

AC-16: The no-stairs way leads when both hold:

- the best no-stairs way costs less than the stairs way + `routing.stairsSaveM`.

The stairs way is then the other way shown (PRD §6.6: "the stairs way is the other way shown"). AC-13's filter does not apply to it, since it is shorter by construction. The labels follow AC-15 ("Recommended", E12). Verify: unit (vectors).

The lead is then "Shortest". Verify: unit (vectors at exactly + 144 m, and with no no-stairs way).

AC-36: At each package load, the worker derives the estimate's two coefficients from the package's own edges (ruling A7, amending L79 RG2's wording; RG2's shape is kept: a planar term plus a per-level term):
- `a = min(1, min over edges with planar run > 0 of (metre cost ÷ planar run))`;
- `b = max(0, min over level-changing edges of ((metre cost − a × planar run) ÷ level span))`, and `b = 0` when no edge changes level;
- the estimate at node n is the minimum over the destination's door nodes d of `a × planar distance(n, d) + b × |ordinal(n) − ordinal(d)|`.

Every edge then satisfies `metre cost ≥ a × planar run + b × level span`. By the triangle inequality in the one venue frame (E10) and on ordinals, two properties follow:
- The estimate is 0 at every door node.
- For every edge u → v, `estimate(u) ≤ metre cost(u → v) + estimate(v)`. The estimate is consistent, so it never exceeds the true remaining cost, whatever costs or weights the mall tunes, and A* never reopens a node.

When a mall's tuning drives both coefficients to 0, the estimate is 0 and A* behaves as Dijkstra: still least-cost, only slower (visible in AC-34's times). Verify: unit (`heuristic_test`):
- each RG2 vector's coefficients equal §4.6's values within 0.001;
- consistency is checked on every edge of every shared vector;
- at every node of every shared vector, the estimate is at most the exact remaining cost from a reverse Dijkstra;
- on V-RG2-1 and V-RG2-2, the literal RG2 estimate is asserted to exceed the exact remaining cost at the named node.

## 5. Scale targets

- **Graph size:** up to about 20,000 nodes per venue, for venues up to about 100,000 m² (PRD §7, "Scale").
- **Mall-scale proof:** the invented Aurora-sized seed, about 400 places on a cross-shaped galleria of five levels with two basements and a car park. It is published through P0-03 "so download, refresh and routing are proven at mall scale before the pilot" (L102; FEATURE_PIPELINE P0-02).
- **Routing latency:** **OPEN, by ruling A5.** r1 and r2 are approved without a latency target.

## 7. Design choices (not requirements; each with an alternative)

- **DC-1 Multi-door destinations:** one search to the set of the place's door nodes. *Alternative:* one search per door, keeping the cheapest; better if places have very few doors and a per-door estimate is much tighter.
- **DC-2 Tie-break:** among equal costs, the lower node index wins. *Alternative:* fewer edges wins; better if the shopper-visible "fewer turns" mattered for equal metres.
- **DC-3 Tolerance:** costs compare within 0.001 m, the database's `DECIMAL(9,3)` resolution. AC-36's consistency check uses the same tolerance. *Alternative:* integer millimetres in the package; better if Spike 2 favours integer edges for size.
- **DC-4 Load check:** the worker refuses a graph with a negative or non-finite cost or a broken CSR, rather than routing on it. *Alternative:* trust P0-03's validation only; acceptable if the package's SHA-256 is the only corruption risk.
- **DC-5 Middle edge:** edge ⌊n/2⌋ (counting from 0) of the lead way's n edges. *Alternative:* the lead way's longest edge; better if the middle edge often sits in a corridor with no parallel.
- **DC-6 Superseding queries:** a newer query replaces a pending one. *Alternative:* queue every query; better if results had to be audited in order (they do not).
- **DC-7 Vector file:** JSON in the repository, read by the Dart tests and by any later server router. *Alternative:* Dart-only fixtures, which fail invariant 6's "shared".
- **DC-8 The leg list:** with directions in r3 (E9), the sheet lists legs: walk N m, "Walk through ⟨store⟩ (N m)", "Take ⟨connector⟩ up/down to ⟨level⟩". Approved with r1 Q2, option 1. *Alternative:* no step list until landmark-first directions; L92's store-crossing step would then not be shown in FF-4.
- **DC-9 "Levels apart" counts level ordinals** (AC-36), the per-level shape A7 keeps. *Alternative:* the levels' height difference in metres (`ElevationM`, FF-1 ruling B6), with b per metre climbed. That keeps the level term across buildings whose ordinals do not line up (V-RG2-6), and is better if such venues prove slow in AC-34's times. Both are never above the true cost.
- **DC-10 a is capped at 1** (FF-4 plan §6.2). A walkway's cost is its length, which is never below its straight-line run, so the cap binds only in a venue with no walkway run. *Alternative:* no cap; equally correct, marginally tighter in that venue.
- **DC-11 A missing or invalid `routing.stairsSaveM` in the package uses the platform default 144**, the same rule P0-04 applies to settings in the mall list. *Alternative:* treat it as an unreadable package (EC-3, routing unavailable); stricter, better if a missing setting should block routing as a corrupt graph does.
- **DC-12 A new Start-here origin during a shown route works the route out again from it**, as AC-26 does for an option. *Alternative:* ignore Start here until ✕; simpler, but it slows testing the tool's snap. The tool is internal only (A1).

**RECALLED, to verify by a doc check in the plan:**
- a long-lived worker made with `Isolate.spawn` and kept alive with `SendPort`/`ReceivePort` (as opposed to the one-shot `Isolate.run` or `compute`);
- `package:sqlite3` opened from a background isolate with its own connection.

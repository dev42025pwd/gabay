## 1. Executive Summary

Gabay is an indoor and whole-site wayfinding app for Philippine shopping malls. Shoppers open the app on their own phone, which is often a budget Android, and see where they are, find any store, restroom or exit, and follow the shortest route there, across floors, wings, parking and outdoor grounds, even without mobile data. Mall-operator companies are the paying clients. They draw and publish their own maps, and they see how their venue is used. **Value: from the entrance to any store by the shortest way, on any phone, with or without signal.**

## 2. Product Vision

Beyond the MVP, Gabay grows into the mall's operating layer:
- live venue status;
- parking and find my car;
- route analytics for mall operators (opt-in only);
- merchant self-service (store status, promos);
- mall-unit tools for guards and emergency teams;
- offline evacuation guidance;
- crowd-aware routing fed by mall-owned sensors (future plans).

**What Gabay will NOT be:**
- It is not an outdoor or city navigation app. Navigation ends at the venue boundary.
- It is not surveyed-fingerprint positioning: no paid collectors re-walking the mall.
- It is not a marketing push engine that targets individuals.
- It does not collect crowd data from shoppers' phones.
- It does not share a shopper's location with responders.
- It is not an emergency or fire-safety system of record.
- Shopper position never leaves the phone, except through opt-in route analytics.

## 4. Problem Statement

Philippine malls are large, multi-level and multi-wing. Their directories are static boards or in-app lists with no "you are here" and no route. Shoppers lose time asking guards or backtracking, and seniors and PWD shoppers cannot tell which route is step-free.
- Indoor mobile coverage has dead spots: "some dead spots remain" at Festival Mall after Globe's 2026 upgrade. SM's free Wi-Fi is capped at 2 hours behind a captive portal.
- The dominant phones are budget Android models: Transsion 33% and Xiaomi 22% of the PH market (Omdia Q2 2026), mostly without a gyroscope or barometer.

**Cost of the problem: measured at the pilot (L109).** B.1 asks for the cost in concrete terms: for example minutes lost per visit, the share of shoppers who ask a guard, and missed store visits. No figure has been stated, and none is invented here. The pilot measures it, before and after Gabay: a short shopper survey (minutes spent finding a store; whether they asked someone) and the guards' and information desk's count of direction questions per day. Until then, the success metrics (plan L36) measure the product, not the pain.

**Why now:** a competitor is piloting at SM North EDSA. BLE beacons, plus on-device routing that works offline, now make a live dot possible on those phones without a paid venue survey.

## 5. Solution Overview

- **Shopper app** (Android and iOS from the pilot, phones and tablets; L103). It:
  - detects the mall when opened, or lets the shopper pick it;
  - downloads the mall's map package once and works offline;
  - shows a live dot (BLE indoors and in covered parking, GPS outdoors);
  - offers "select where you are" when the dot is unsure;
  - finds stores and amenities;
  - routes by the shortest path by default, with one other way shown (L86, L87);
  - looks and behaves as the demo app shows (L85): a tilted 2.5D map, a search bar with "nearest" chips, level chips, place and route sheets, an emergency button (hidden until evacuation guidance ships, §6.17, L117), and the tabs Places / Map / Parking / Me.
- **Admin web** (Flutter Web). Mall admins:
  - build the whole site floor by floor in a dimensioned editor (grid boxes, polygons, traced floor plans, CAD/IMDF import later);
  - get a BLE placement proposal from the model;
  - publish immutable map versions;
  - manage roles, and later live status, the analytics dashboard (the spike's, totals only; L104) and visit forecasts (L105).
- **Platform** (IQ): tenants, beacon namespaces, global reference data, and isolation between tenants.

## 6. Feature Requirements & Prioritization

### 6.1 Venue, buildings & levels (Priority: High)
**Description.** An admin defines a venue as the whole mall property: its buildings and wings, parking structures, outdoor ground, and the venue boundary.
**Acceptance Criteria.**
- An admin can create a venue with a boundary polygon, then add buildings (main, wing, annex, parking) and levels with real dimensions in metres.
- An outdoor ground level can be added, outside any building.
- A nested facility (for example a department store) can hold its own interior levels and sections.
- From P1, the admin can say how many buildings there are and how many levels each has, then upload a plan for each level to generate a draft (§6.22, L108).
- An admin sets each building's "up": the angle shown at the top of the shopper's map when it is not turning, to match the mall's own directory boards. The venue's angle covers the outdoor ground and any building without one (L111).
- An admin can give a store, a level or a lift, escalator or stairs an optional "spoken as" (for example "UG" as "Upper Ground"), which the voice uses instead of the written name (L112).
- Every change is attributable in the audit trail.
**Out of scope:** areas beyond the venue boundary.

### 6.2 Map editor (Priority: High)
**Description.** A dimensioned floor editor that admins can use in whichever way they prefer:
- grid boxes with an object library;
- free polygons;
- tracing over an uploaded floor plan.

The walkable area and the routing graph are derived from what is drawn.
**Acceptance Criteria.**
- An admin sets the floor size and grid cell size in metres and places objects by dimension: a 9×9 m store appears as 9×9 m on a 40×40 m floor.
- Any placed box or polygon can be moved, resized and rotated.
- An uploaded floor-plan image can be calibrated with two points and a known distance, then traced.
- Stairs and escalators are placed with a height, and elevators with the floors they serve. Each escalator is set one-way (in its real direction) or two-way, as the mall really is (L86).
- Each mall can tune the metre cost of each connector type (L86).
- Doors carry a clear width, so step-free routes can avoid narrow ones (L88).
- For evacuation, the admin marks refuge areas, assembly areas and evacuation-only stairs, and whether escalators may be used (L87).
- At publish, the walkway graph is generated automatically. An admin can block, add or remove a path.
- An admin assigns a store to a unit (name, category, logo, search keywords) and places amenities and transit points (L29, L30). After publish, each is found by §6.5's search.
- An admin marks a store as walk-through when shoppers may cross it from one door to another (L29, L92).
- A mall admin sets each store's status (§6.5). The status list is a lookup the mall can extend (L83).
- A Venue Editor can save drafts but cannot publish.
- A draft generated from an uploaded plan (§6.22, L108) opens here; its unreviewed elements are listed and highlighted until each is confirmed or edited.
**Out of scope:** CAD/IMDF import and drafts from uploaded plans, which are their own P1 feature (§6.22).

### 6.3 Publish (Priority: High)
**Description.** Publishing turns a draft into an immutable, versioned venue package that shoppers download.
**Acceptance Criteria.**
- Publish is refused, with the reason named, if any of these fail: every store reachable; no orphan paths; connectors paired; everything inside the boundary; no beacon identity collision; no walkway, generated or admin-corrected, crossing a wall (L92); any element generated from an uploaded plan still unreviewed (L108). A wall crossing, and each unreviewed element, is shown on the map.
- Publish works out which walk-through stores are the only way to a place, and marks them as passages for those places (L92).
- ~~Publish generates a "You are here" code for every place and a print sheet (L99, L101).~~ Removed by L107: the product has no QR codes.
- Publish warns, without blocking, about coverage gaps and near-collinear beacon placement.
- A published version is never edited. A correction is a new version.
- A shopper holding the previous version keeps it until the new one is verified, and switches to it only between trips (§6.4, L102).
**Out of scope:** partial or live edits to a published version.

### 6.4 Shopper shell & package delivery (Priority: High)
**Description.** The shopper app finds the mall, downloads its package, and stays usable offline.
**Acceptance Criteria.**
- Opening the app inside a mall's boundary preselects that mall. The shopper can also pick any mall manually.
- The package downloads once. While online and in the foreground, the app checks for a newer version on launch, when it detects or the shopper picks the venue, and every few minutes (a setting, default 5 min; L102). A newer version downloads in the background and replaces the old one only when no route or evacuation is active; a shopper mid-route finishes on the version they started with, and the sheet then says "Map updated". Offline shoppers keep what they have.
- Every map comes from the database (L102): the mall's staff edit a draft in the editor (§6.2), publish makes it an immutable version (§6.3), and the app ships no venue inside its own build. Only store status and closures reach shoppers without a republish (§6.14); any other change, including a tenant swap, is a new published version.
- With no data, or behind a captive portal, every feature in §6.5–§6.9 still works.
- The map follows the demo app (L85):
  - a tilted 2.5D view by default, with a flat toggle;
  - pinch to zoom (no zoom buttons), and two-finger rotate with a compass button that returns to the mall's own "up" (§6.1; L111 amends "returns to north"; the needle still shows north where the venue is georeferenced);
  - recentre and set-location buttons;
  - level chips that show where you are and which levels the route uses;
  - the whole-site view, the all-levels stacked view, and wing shortcuts;
  - labels that thin out as you zoom out.
- The scene is drawn from the published venue: store slabs, walls with door gaps, and column and furniture blocks. The dot, route and pins are always on top, and pin tap targets are at least 48 dp.
- The route sheet slides between one line, a summary and the full steps. Swiping never ends a route; only ✕ does.
- The tabs are Places, Map, Parking and Me.
- The red emergency button is hidden until evacuation guidance (§6.17) ships for that mall (L117); the shell differs from the demo's look until then.
- Devices (L103): phones and tablets on Android 7.0 or newer and iOS 15 or newer, both released at the pilot. Phones are portrait only, as the demo. A tablet gets a wide layout, with the map and the sheet side by side, and may turn to landscape (Blueprint DC11). A phone without Bluetooth LE, or with Bluetooth or location denied, keeps every feature except the live dot (§6.8).
- Me holds wording (Simple or Technical), light or dark, the wheelchair route, "Shortcuts through stores" (Off or Allow, off by default; L92), and the "For everyone" settings (L99): Language (English or everyday Tagalog), Voice guidance (off by default), Vibrate before each turn (on), Text size (Normal, Larger, Largest), High contrast, Walking pace (Normal, Relaxed, Slow), Avoid stairs, Avoid escalators, Prefer stairs, Map faces the way I walk (off by default; L111), and the Wrong-way sound (off by default). Each is specified in §6.6 and §6.23.
- The role picker, Test tools and the demo panel exist only in internal builds, never in a shopper's release build (L85).
- The venue picker, wording, look, wheelchair mode, the shortcut setting, every §6.23 setting, favourites, the last 8 destinations, the parked car, the camera and the last venue are saved on the phone across restarts, and never sent (L88, L99). A broken or foreign saved value falls back to the default. An active route is not restored.
**Out of scope:** background downloads when the app is closed.

### 6.5 Directory & search (Priority: High)
**Description.** Search stores, amenities and transit points as you type, including Filipino usage ("CR", "comfort room").
**Acceptance Criteria.**
- Typing "cr" or "comfort room" lists restrooms, and a "Nearest CR" option routes to the closest one. "Closest" means the shortest walking distance (metres, including the connector costs) from the shopper, honouring step-free mode and one-way connectors (L79 RG1, L86).
- One walking-distance search from the shopper's spot feeds every distance on screen, as in the demo (L103): search results are listed closest first, with places that can't be reached last; every place shows "About N m to walk"; the chips, the Parking tab's zones and the Places list use the same figures. It is redone when the shopper moves to another walkway point, or changes step-free, shortcuts or an avoid setting, or a closure changes. The start point is a walkway the shopper can reach without crossing a wall (L79 RG3), not simply the straight-line nearest point.
- Results appear within 100 ms on the reference budget phone.
- Nested sections (for example "Shoes" inside the department store) are searchable.
- Each store shows its mall-set status: Open, Closed, Being repaired or Opening soon, plus any the mall adds (L83). A store that isn't open has a dimmed pin and a disabled Go, with the reason shown, as in the mock-up.
- "Nearest" chips on the map are Restroom, ATM, Food, Lift, Exit, EV charging, Parking, My car and Stairs. Each lists the 3 nearest open places by walking distance (L85); Stairs lists the nearest staircases on this floor with the floors they serve (L99).
- The Places tab lists places by floor, grouped by category, with the facilities and the lifts, escalators, stairs and ramps that stop there (L85).
- A star on every place marks a favourite. Favourites and the last 8 destinations (newest first, no repeats) top an empty search and the Places tab. They stay on the phone and are never sent (L99).
- Voice search (L99): a microphone in the search bar and in "Where are you?" listens for one phrase in English or Filipino. "Nearest restroom" starts the route to the nearest one straight away, with the others listed (L112, the spike's way); a shop name searches and reads back the top three; "the first one" / "una" picks it. Without a recogniser or permission, the button explains. **Where the audio goes (L116):** recognition on the phone is asked for first; where the phone can't (often for Filipino), the first tap asks "Your phone sends this to Google/Apple to understand it. Use it?" with Yes and Type instead, and the answer is remembered on the phone. The microphone listens only after the tap, for one phrase (§7). Gabay stops speaking while it listens (L112). The reply is spoken even when Voice guidance is off, but not when the voice is muted (L112).
**Out of scope:** product-level inventory search.

### 6.6 Routing (Priority: High)
**Description.** The app recommends the shortest route to the destination, across floors, wings, parking and outdoor areas, and shows one other way beside it (L86, L87).
**Acceptance Criteria.**
- **Shortest by default (L86):**
  - The default route is the shortest in metres.
  - Each floor change adds its connector type's metre cost, a setting each mall can tune. The default is the demo's height-based formula (L91): lift 30 + 1.5 per metre climbed, stairs 34 + 3, escalator 14 + 1, ramp 20 + 2.
  - The time to arrive is the route's metre cost at 1.2 m/s.
  - One-way escalators are used only in their direction.
  - **Stairs only when they save time (L99, amending L86):** a route that takes stairs is the default only when it beats the best no-stairs route by the stairs threshold, a setting each mall can tune (default 144 m of cost, about two minutes at 1.2 m/s). Otherwise the lift or escalator route is the default and the stairs way is the other way shown. "Prefer stairs" in Me makes the plain shortest way win. Wheelchair routes, Avoid stairs and evacuations are unchanged.
- **One other way, unasked (L87):**
  - Every route also shows one other way: a dotted amber line on the map and "Shortest / Other way 1" in the sheet.
  - It avoids the main route's connectors (or its middle stretch), and is shown only if it is longer than the main route by more than 0.5 m and less than twice its length.
- **Shortcuts through stores (L92):**
  - A route cuts through a store only if the mall has marked it walk-through, it is open (§6.5), and the shopper has allowed "Shortcuts through stores" in Me. The setting is off by default.
  - The route goes in by one door, across the store's floor and out by another. The walk across counts 1.25× its length, a setting each mall can tune, so only a clearly shorter shortcut wins. The steps show the real metres.
  - A marked store that is the only way to a place is a passage, not a shortcut. Routes use it whatever the setting (§6.3).
  - The step reads "Walk through *store* (*m*)", with the store's icon.
  - Changing the setting works an active route out again.
- **Turn-by-turn directions, landmark first (L99):** each direction names a landmark before metres ("Turn left at Zen Gifts · in 20 m · past the ATM"); a big card with an arrow sits in the route sheet, the full list under it. A bend of 30° or more is a turn; a slight bend (30–50°) counts only at a fork where another walkway leaves and is about as straight; a corridor that merely curves gets no instruction. Floor changes and the connectors used are named. The metres count down as the shopper walks.
- **Voice guidance (L99; off by default):** the phone's text-to-speech in English (Philippines) or Filipino, through Gabay's own platform channel, worded like a person ("Walk straight towards Zen Gifts, then turn left"; "Walk past the ATM towards the escalator, then take it up to level 2"). A turn within 25 m of the one before is said with it; metres only where there is nothing to name; "Coming up: …" about 20 m before a turn announced more than 40 m back, and "Turn left now" about 5 m before it (L112; settings, tuned in the field); off a ride, the new level and what should be around. Sentences queue and are never cut off; only alerts, the emergency, arriving and answers interrupt; a stale cue is dropped, not said late. **Priority (L112):** the emergency, then a closure on the route, arrival, answers, checks, then directions; the emergency speaks and buzzes three times even with the voice off or muted. A speaker button sits on the rail while a route is active: it is the same saved switch as Me → Voice guidance, and turning it on speaks the direction ahead at once (L112). Tapping the direction card says the current direction again (L112). Browsing the ways around says nothing; only "Go this way" speaks the new first direction (the demo speaks each one; not copied). On a walk over 40 m there are checkpoints every 25–40 m, then "Getting close now", "Almost there" and "This should be it", each once. Stairs and escalators say each landing; an express escalator only the floors it stops at; a lift nothing between.
- **Suggestive wording and checks (L99, amended by L113):** anything about where the shopper is or what they will see is always worded suggestively, whatever the dot ("You should be on Level 2 now, with Jollibee on your left"); instructions stay plain ("Turn left at Jollibee"). When the position is hand-set, approximate or unsure (§6.8, §6.9), pausing asks "Are you here? …" with Yes / No, I'm somewhere else; reaching a lift, escalator or stairs asks "Are you at ⟨connector⟩?" once per ride; and at the end "You should be at ⟨place⟩ now. Tap I've arrived if you are." The confetti and "You have arrived" wait for that tap. With a confident live dot (§6.9) there is no check at the foot of a ride, and arrival is detected as below, with "I've arrived" still offered. **After every ride, whatever the dot (L113):** stepping off asks "Are you on ⟨level⟩? You should see ⟨A⟩ on your ⟨side⟩ …" with Yes / No, I'm somewhere else; the question stays on the card while guidance continues, and No opens "Where are you?". Questions open as a card over the map, never hidden in a swiped-down sheet. "No, I'm somewhere else" opens "Where are you?" (§6.8).
- **Pause (L99):** a full-width Pause / Resume under the direction card (a pause square on the rail while the sheet is down). The check and the resume name one landmark and the one next thing to do, never the directions after it.
- **Vibrate before each turn (L99; on by default):** about 20 m before each turn and ride, one short buzz for left, two for right, one long before a lift, escalator or stairs, three for an alert; once each.
- **Map faces the way I walk (L99, L111; off by default):**
  - During a route only, the map turns to the heading of the route segment the shopper is on (not the compass). Turning the phone while standing still changes nothing.
  - Bends under about 20° are ignored; at a real turn the map glides the short way round in about 0.6 s (settings), or turns in one step when the phone's animations are off.
  - It holds its angle while riding, then turns to the new floor's first segment; it holds while the dot is unsure or grey; a hand-set position faces the route's first segment from there.
  - Any pan, pinch or twist pauses it and follow-me; Recentre resumes both.
  - During a route the rail shows a "face my way" button, and the first route ever shows a one-time tip pointing at it. The compass button switches the turning off for this route only (the Me setting is unchanged); tapping it again turns it back on.
  - The same on phones and tablets, portrait or landscape, tilted or flat.
- **Left and right (L113):** always as the shopper walks along the route, with a landmark where there is one; never "up" or "down" the screen, whether or not the map turns.
- **Guided walk, the fallback when the live dot fails (P0, P0-15; L118):**
  - "Guide me step by step" on the route sheet starts it, by hand only. The dot moves along the route at the shopper's walking pace (§6.23) and **waits at every turn and landmark**, asking "Are you at ⟨landmark⟩? Turn left here." Yes carries it to the next one; No opens "Where are you?" (§6.8) and the route is redone. It is never more than one stretch ahead.
  - Slower and faster buttons on the card adjust the pace, remembered on the phone; Pause holds it.
  - The first route ever shows a one-time tip pointing at it. Whenever the live dot can't be trusted (Bluetooth off or denied, no beacons heard, a grey dot), the card shows a quiet hint, "Can't find you? Try Guide me step by step", without starting it.
  - A guided position is not live-confident: the wording is suggestive, rides get the check at their foot and the floor question after, and arrival is by the "I've arrived" tap. Wrong turns are caught by the shopper's No; the off-route rule is off (Blueprint DC26). The voice and the turning map follow the guided dot.
  - If the live dot comes back confident, a card asks "Your position is back. Use it?" with Yes and Keep guiding.
- **Preview the route (L118):** a Preview button on the route sheet plays the route on the map with a ghost dot at about 3×, the map turning and no voice. It never moves the shopper's position. The demo panel itself stays in internal builds only (L85).
- **Am I on the right path? (P1, P1-06; L113):** a button on the direction card during any route, with any dot. A tap pauses guidance; the card (and the voice, if on) says "Stop here a moment. You should be near ⟨place⟩, with ⟨A⟩ on your left and ⟨B⟩ ahead. Is that what you see?" with Yes, carry on and No, I'm somewhere else. No opens "Where are you?" (§6.8), which recalibrates and re-routes.
- A route starts from the shopper's position, joined to the nearest walkway they can reach without crossing a wall (L79 RG3).
- **Find another way (L87):**
  - During a trip, the shopper can tap "Find another way" at any time.
  - The ways around avoid the next ~30 m of the route, or the next lift, escalator or stairs taken as a whole. The 30 m is a setting.
  - The sheet lists "Your current way" and up to 3 ways around. Ways that overlap too much with one already listed are dropped.
  - Each way shows its metres and minutes, plus "+X m · +Y min", "Same length", the number of level changes and "Goes outside".
  - "Go this way" switches to the chosen way. Cancel keeps the current way.
  - The sheet also lists similar places nearby, fixed tips, and "Get help", which routes to the nearest information desk.
  - If no way around exists, it says so and offers similar places.
  - If the way ahead cuts through a store, the ways around avoid the whole store, and the tips add "If a store's door is locked or closed, the other ways go around the store." (L92)
- **Venue alerts (L87):**
  - When a staff closure or outage lies on the route ahead, the app shows it on the map, under the route ("Problem on this way") and on the ways that pass it ("Passes the reported problem").
  - It opens the other-ways sheet. Nothing is avoided automatically: the shopper keeps going or picks another way.
- **Off route (L80):**
  - A deviation is confirmed only after 3 consecutive position updates beyond a named limit, never on a single bad position. The limit is 5 m from the route in corridors and 7 m inside a store, where positioning is weaker (settings; L110, from the design notes).
  - Once confirmed, the directions refresh and the app says "You may be going the wrong way". With the Wrong-way sound on (§6.23), the short clip plays first, then "Looks like you've drifted off the way. Let's start again from here.", and "Where are you?" is offered (L99). The same happens when the shopper answers "No, I'm somewhere else" to a check.
  - The same notice shows when progress along the route falls for 3 updates by more than a named limit.
  - A refresh keeps the trip's "another way" avoidance.
  - The route never changes on its own otherwise: only after a confirmed wrong turn, or when the shopper picks another way (L110).
- **Browsing (L110):** a shopper who stops inside a store while on a route is browsing, not lost. Guidance goes quiet: no wrong-way notice, sound, vibration or re-route. After about 2 minutes (a setting) a small card at the bottom asks "Still going to ⟨destination⟩?" with Continue and Pause; it makes no sound or vibration, and ignoring it changes nothing. Walking out of the store towards the route resumes guidance by itself, with one short line ("Back on the way to ⟨destination⟩").
- **Naming the other ways (L110, from the design notes):** the other way, and each way around, is named by what makes it different: "via Elevator B", "via the north escalators", "past the Bookstore", "goes outside".
- The avoidance stays on the phone, is never sent, and is cleared when the trip ends (L80).
- The trip ends on arrival, or when the shopper taps Stop.
  - The shopper can also tap "I've arrived". When the position is hand-set, approximate or unsure, arrival is only by that tap (L99, hedged wording above).
  - Arriving gives a short double vibration and about 2.5 s of confetti. During an evacuation, or when the phone's animations are off, there is only the vibration (L85).
  - **Open item:** arrival is detected when the position stays within a named distance of the destination's door for a named time. The values are set from testing, and the spike's 2.0 m for 2 s (plan L66) is only the starting point. **L117:** it is the provisional setting until the field test replaces it.
**Out of scope:** routes that leave the venue boundary.

### 6.7 Step-free routing (Priority: High)
**Description.** A step-free mode for seniors, PWD shoppers and anyone with a stroller.
**Acceptance Criteria.**
- With step-free on ("Wheelchair route: lifts only"), no route uses stairs or escalators, and no route uses a walkway or door narrower than 1.2 m (a setting). Publish computes each walkway's clearance (L88, closing RG4).
- Ramps and elevators are used, and accessible CRs are offered first.
- Separate from step-free mode, "Avoid stairs" and "Avoid escalators" in Me are strict (L99): no route uses the avoided kind. When nothing else reaches a place, the route uses it and says so. Evacuations ignore both.
- Step-free routes never take a shortcut through a store, whatever the setting. A store that is the only way to a place is used, and its doors are held to the same 1.2 m (L92).
- If no step-free route exists, the app says so, offers "Turn off wheelchair route", and never shows a stepped route on its own.
**Out of scope:** guarantees beyond the clearances the venue geometry gives.

### 6.8 Set your location (Priority: High)
**Description.** Whenever the live dot is unavailable or unsure, the shopper can set where they are.
**Acceptance Criteria.**
- "Where are you?" opens on entering a venue and from the pin button. It searches shops, entrances and lifts, and places the shopper at the chosen place's door.
- The shopper can describe where they are ("near the ATM beside the escalator", "malapit sa CR"): any word that names a place or a facility counts, this floor first, and places by a named lift, escalator or stairs are listed as "near Escalator E1" (L99). The same works by voice (§6.5).
- The shopper can instead pick just their floor. This gives an approximate dot (drawn with a dashed ring), which a tap on the map refines (L85).
- A new position recalculates an active route from there.
- **No QR codes (L107).** The anchor codes (L16), the place codes (L99) and the scan reset (L101) are removed; a known spot comes only from search, description, a tap or a floor pick.
- The first live-confident position takes over from a place chosen by search, description or floor pick (L106). During a guided walk (§6.6, L118) it is offered, not taken: "Your position is back. Use it?"
- A place chosen after describing where you are (typed or spoken, then picked from the suggestions) also calibrates the phone (§6.9, L107).
- All of this works with Bluetooth and location permission denied.
**Out of scope:** QR codes of any kind (L107).

### 6.9 Live position (Priority: High)
**Description.** A live dot that appears on its own and follows the shopper, becoming the default starting point for every search.
**Acceptance Criteria.**
- Inside a mall, with the app open, the first live position appears without any tap. The target is 5 s or less, set from the measurement spikes.
- The dot follows at least once per second, snapped to walkways, and holds still when the shopper stands still.
- **How the dot moves, as the spike does (L106; gabay_spike L67, L76, L100; every value a setting, tuned in Spike 1):**
  - a jump of more than 2.5 m is not followed until 3 fixes agree, and meanwhile the dot moves at most 0.25 m per fix;
  - it is smoothed over 0.5 s while walking and 4 s while still, and never moves faster than 1.8 m/s;
  - it glides to each new position in about half a second at walking pace, along the walkway, never across a wall;
  - it sits on the nearest walkway it can reach without crossing a wall, and leaves it only inside a store or restroom or outside every room; on a route within 2 m it sits at the shopper's progress along the route. Whether the shopper is off the route is judged on the unsnapped position, so the snap never hides a wrong turn;
  - it freezes once the phone has been still for about 1 s (D4b #4), and moves again on a step, or after 5 updates more than 3 m away.
- **How the dot looks (L106):** a solid soft halo sized by its accuracy (the dashed ring stays for approximate and hand-set positions, §6.8); a cone in the demo's look pointing where the shopper is actually walking, hidden on arrival and while riding, fading out when they stop; "You are in ⟨store⟩" inside an open store; "On the escalator" while riding; and, when the shopper views another level, a faded dot with a floor tag where they are (this differs from the demo, by ruling).
- **Unsure and grey (L106):** above 4 m of uncertainty the dot is "unsure" and labelled "Near ⟨place⟩", until it is under 3 m for 3 updates. At 6 m it greys out, and the app offers "select where you are".
- The dot's state drives the checks (L99, amended by L113): a confident live dot gets detected arrival and no check at the foot of a ride; a greyed, hand-set or approximate position gets the checks of §6.6. Places and surroundings are worded suggestively either way, and every ride ends with the floor question of §6.6.
- Floor changes show only after an escalator, stair or lift (D4b #8; the QR route is removed, L107). If, away from any connector, another floor's beacons dominate for about 15 s (a setting), the dot turns unsure and asks "Are you on ⟨level⟩?": yes moves it, no keeps it (L106). After a ride, the floor question of §6.6 stands in for this check (L113, Blueprint DC25).
- **Calibration (L107, replacing L101's QR trigger):** when the shopper picks their place after describing it (typed or spoken), the beacons heard there are compared with the signal expected at that spot, and a per-device offset is computed, clamped, averaged, saved on the phone and applied afterwards; never sent. A pick counts in proportion to how small the place is, and a large place does not count (Blueprint DC18).
- Outdoors, GPS is used; in covered parking and indoors, beacons are used.
- In venues with sentinels, mall admins see each beacon's last sentinel sighting and battery-service date, and a "Silent" state (from the plan's live-position criteria).
- No position is sent to the server unless the shopper opted in to analytics (§6.19). Whenever the live dot can't be trusted, the card hints at the guided walk (§6.6, L118).
**Out of scope:** detection while the app is closed.

### 6.17 Evacuation guidance (Priority: Low)
**Description.** Offline nearest-safe-exit guidance that any shopper can start from the red emergency button by the search bar, as in the demo app (L87). **Tier P3 (L117):** the button is hidden in the shell until this ships for a mall whose safety officer and fire-code review have signed off.
**Acceptance Criteria.**
- Guidance works offline from the published package.
- The route goes to the nearest open exit. Lifts are never used. Whether escalators may be used is the mall's setting (the demo allows them).
- Evacuation-only stairs (for example to basements) are used only in an evacuation.
- An evacuation never takes a shortcut through a store. A store that is the only way out is used (L92); the L27 review covers this.
- Wheelchair users go to the nearest exit on their level, or else to the mall's refuge area, to wait for staff. The demo's default refuge is a stair landing.
- Outdoors, the app sends the shopper to the mall's assembly area.
- "This exit is blocked" reroutes to the next exit.
- "Path blocked? Mark it" lets the shopper tap a blocked corridor or connector. The evacuation goes around it, on that phone only.
- "Get help" shows the staff note, and "Stop evacuation" ends it. Back never ends it, and no other trip can replace it.
- No shopper location is shared.
- The feature ships only after the mall's safety officer and a fire-code review sign off (L27). Its refuge areas, assembly areas, escalator use and evacuation-only stairs are set by each mall (L87).
- Only the shopper starts it, as in the demo. The mall cannot switch shoppers' phones into evacuation mode (L91).
**Out of scope:** acting as the mall's official alarm or evacuation system.

### 6.19 Route analytics & consent (Priority: High)
**Description.** Mall admins (and later merchants) see how the venue is used: routes, most-visited and most-searched stores, peak hours, and how many shoppers navigated to each store.
**Acceptance Criteria.**
- Route traces are collected only from shoppers who opted in through a separate, just-in-time prompt. The default is off.
- Withdrawing consent takes one tap and stops collection immediately.
- Traces are pseudonymised, and a merchant sees only their own store's figures.
- The feature ships only after a DPO, counsel and the NPC registration check clear it. **L116:** the product owner is the DPO; counsel reviews the privacy notice, the consent screen and the mall data-processing agreement before it ships; NPC registration is not mandatory under the Circular 2022-04 thresholds, and the DPO is registered voluntarily before the pilot.
- **The dashboard follows the spike's (L104; gabay_spike L71, the profile APK of 2026-10-06), in the admin web page:**
  - a venue picker and filters for dates, destination and map version (the current version by default);
  - a map of where people walked: a heat map in 1 m cells and the busiest walkways, floor by floor;
  - the headline numbers: visits to the mall, routes started, the typical time to arrive, and the share of routes that left the path;
  - visits per day and by hour of the day; where people went; the busiest walkways;
  - the funnel from looking up to arriving: card opened, started walking, arrived, said yes ("said yes" only on trips whose position was unsure, L99);
  - a store list (search; visits, cards opened, and how many went on to walk there) and a page per store: visits and arrivals per day or week, the typical and the long stay inside, its own funnel, and visits before and after each status change; and the 5 busiest stores compared.
- It shows **totals only** (L104): no individual's path, no per-person filter or list. A figure built from fewer than 5 shoppers is hidden (`analytics.minGroupN` = 5, a setting; L116, after PDPC's "5 or more for external data sharing").
- All of it comes from shoppers who opted in, including the counts of cards opened and routes started, and says so.
- Figures are added up nightly; the dashboard shows up to yesterday.
- A visit, a stay, a door-zone "estimated" visit and the status window use the spike's rules, kept as mall settings with the spike's values as defaults (Blueprint §4.11).
- Guided-walk and preview movement is never recorded: it is not a real position (L118, Blueprint DC27).
- The trace now covers the whole visit while the dot shows, not only the route, so the consent text must say so.
**Out of scope:** selling or sharing data outside the tenant.

### 6.20 BLE placement planner (Priority: High)
**Description.** From the finished floor model, Gabay proposes where to install beacons.
**Acceptance Criteria.**
- For each floor the planner offers three plans: minimum proximity, recommended tracking and budget-capped.
- Each plan shows positions, mount heights, count, estimated cost and a coverage map. Mount heights follow L45: the highest point available, and the ceiling where it is below 3 m.
- Connector landings and entrances always get beacons.
- Before a floor is drawn, and beside each plan as a sanity check, the planner shows a rule-of-thumb estimate: ⌈A × k / s²⌉ + E beacons and their cost, from the floor area, an accuracy tier (15 / 10 / 6.5 m spacing) and a wall factor (1.0 / 1.3 / 1.8), plus one per entrance, connector landing and corridor junction, never fewer than 4 per floor (L95). Corridors count by area like any walkable unit (L96).
- All counts, the estimate's and the plans', are budgeting figures until a walk survey of the installed floor confirms them before the fleet order. Who walks it and when is the pilot's call (M6, L96).
- The chosen plan feeds the beacon registry.
**Out of scope:** installing or buying beacons; CAD/IMDF import for the editor, which is its own P1 feature (P1-03).

### 6.22 CAD/IMDF import, and drafts from uploaded plans (Priority: High)
**Description.** The fourth L39 editor mode, at P1 (P1-03). An admin imports a DXF or IMDF file into a floor as a starting point, then edits it like anything drawn by hand. **L108 widens it:** the admin sets the buildings and the levels of each (§6.1), uploads one plan per level, and Gabay generates an unpublished draft map from it by rules, with no AI. The admin reviews and corrects the draft, then publishes; later mall changes are ordinary edits and republishes.
**Acceptance Criteria.**
- An admin can upload a DXF or IMDF file for a floor, and its shapes appear in the editor at real scale in metres.
- Imported shapes are ordinary editor shapes: each can be moved, resized and rotated (L39).
- Importing never publishes; publishing still runs the §6.3 checks.
- **Accepted plans (L108):** DXF and IMDF (read directly), vector PDF (its line paths and text labels), and scanned or raster plans (PNG, JPG, a scanned PDF). A directory-board photo is not generated from; it can only be traced as an underlay (§6.2).
- **What is generated (L108):** walls, rooms (closed regions between walls; the largest connected one becomes the concourse), doors (wall gaps of 0.7–1.5 m, confirmed by a swing arc on scans) and, where the file names them, stairs, escalators and lifts. Names come from the file's text (DXF, IMDF, vector PDF); on a scan, rooms come out unnamed and the admin names them, picking from the mall's store list. No OCR and no AI are used.
- **Scale and floors (L108):** a scan is scaled with two points and a known distance, as in tracing (§6.2). The levels of a building are lined up by clicking the same two fixed features on each (for example a lift shaft and a column).
- **Review gate (L108):** every generated wall, room, door and connector starts as "unreviewed"; the ones the rules were unsure of are highlighted. Publish is refused until each is confirmed or edited.
- **Not generated:** escalator direction, lift details, walk-through stores, step-free widths beyond what was measured, store details, and beacons. These are set in the editor and the placement planner (§6.20).
- **Privacy (L108):** the plan is processed in the admin's browser, so it is not sent anywhere to be processed. The original file is kept (as a `CAD_IMPORT` or `UNDERLAY` file) only if the admin chooses (Blueprint DC21).
- **Drafted for a later revision, not built now (L108; P3-04):** assisted tools in the traced-plan mode: click inside a room to fill it to its walls; snap to the plan's detected walls while drawing; click a detected wall gap to make a door of its measured width.
**Out of scope:** AI or OCR recognition; generating from photos of directory boards; file formats other than DXF, IMDF, PDF, PNG and JPG.

### 6.23 Language & accessibility (Priority: High)
**Description.** The demo's "For everyone" settings (L99): everyone can read, hear and feel the guidance.
**Acceptance Criteria.**
- **Language:** English or everyday Tagalog for all everyday wording and the voice. Shop and level names stay as the mall wrote them, spoken through the optional "spoken as" where the mall set one (§6.1, L112); Technical wording stays English. The Tagalog is everyday Taglish as people say it in malls: mall words stay English (escalator, elevator, CR, level), and store names are never translated (L112). Every everyday phrase has a Tagalog version, and a test checks it. The product owner reviews and approves the Tagalog wording before the P0 app ships (L109); the demo's wording is the draft.
- **Text size:** Normal, Larger or Largest (×1.0, ×1.2, ×1.4) on top of the phone's font size, map labels included; nothing overflows at the largest size.
- **High contrast:** pure text colours, darker outlines, and a dashed other way so colour is never the only difference; text and the route label read at 4.5:1 or better.
- **Walking pace:** Normal, Relaxed or Slow (×1.0, ×1.3, ×1.7) changes the minutes shown and, since L118, the guided walk's starting pace (1.2 m/s divided by the factor); nothing else.
- **Screen reader:** every button has a name; the level chips say "you are here"; the direction card is a live region that says the sentence, not the metres; the map describes itself (level, route, next direction). When TalkBack or VoiceOver is on, Gabay's own voice stays quiet and the screen reader reads the direction card, so only one voice speaks; vibration stays, and the Voice guidance setting says why (L112).
- **Vibration, voice and the wrong-way sound** are specified in §6.6. The wrong-way sound is off by default; when the voice is muted nothing plays and nothing is said, but "Where are you?" still opens and the screen reader gets the line. Gabay records its own clip before release, so it owns it outright (L109); the demo's clip is never shipped.
- **The voice and the phone's audio (L112):**
  - the phone's own speech rate and the media volume; other audio, such as music, is lowered while a sentence plays, then restored;
  - speech goes where the phone sends media (earphones or Bluetooth when connected) and plays in silent mode, since the shopper turned it on; if earphones disconnect during a route, the voice mutes until turned on again;
  - guidance, buzzes and checks run only while Gabay is on screen (the screen stays on during a route); on return, one line says the next direction;
  - with the language on Tagalog and no Filipino voice on the phone, the first time the card offers "Install it" (the phone's voice settings) or "Speak English"; until one is installed, the voice speaks English while the screen stays in Tagalog.
- Every setting here is saved on the phone and never sent (§6.4).
- Permissions: the microphone is requested at the first voice search, explained in place; denying it leaves every other feature working (Apple 5.1.1(iv)). Checks are answered by tap only; answering by voice waits for the DPO's ruling (OQ11; L112). The app asks for no camera permission: QR scanning was removed (L107).
**Out of scope:** languages other than English and Tagalog in the first release.

## 7. Technical Requirements & Constraints

- **Deployment posture (L98):** cloud-hosted on Firebase/Google Cloud in Singapore, one deployment for many mall operators: the admin web page on Firebase Hosting (L93), the API as one Cloud Function (Express inside, capped at one instance for the pilot), the database on Cloud SQL for PostgreSQL through Firebase SQL Connect, files in Cloud Storage, and sign-in by Firebase Auth, which runs in US data centres. D10a and D10b are superseded. The admin page and the shopper app call the API at its own address; the admin live-status stream is served there too. The shopper app is offline-capable per venue package. The §1 deviations this brings (EXCEPTIONS E-06 to E-09) are approved by the product owner; the lead's countersignature is open. **Local first (L109):** until the product owner says otherwise, everything runs on the developer's machine (the Firebase Emulator Suite and a local PostgreSQL). There are no cloud previews; the first Firebase deploy, and every live deploy after it, happens only on the product owner's call.
- **Stack:** mandated by the Dynamiq standard §1. Pins and the allow and deny lists are in Blueprint Part 1.
- **Supported devices (L103):** Android 7.0 (API 24) or newer and iOS 15 or newer, Flutter 3.47's own floor (docs.flutter.dev, checked 2026-10-07); the shopper packages need no more. Phones and tablets; Android and iOS both at the pilot. The iOS resources (a Mac and the Apple account) are in hand (L109). Tablets are for shoppers only; there is no kiosk mode (L109).
